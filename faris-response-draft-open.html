02Audited financials through 2023 exclude delivery fees from gross; management financials from 2024 include them. Useful to see 2020–2025 like-for-like. Following

Agreed, and the inconsistency you identified is real. In the audited statements for 2023 and earlier, delivery fee income was not recognised as a separate revenue stream — delivery income and delivery cost were netted against each other and reported together within cost of sales. From 2024, delivery fees are recognised as their own revenue line. That change in treatment, rather than any shift in the underlying business, is what makes the reported series discontinuous.

We have restated every year from 2020 onto a single consistent basis: delivery fees presented as revenue throughout, revenue reported net of discounts and refunds.

03What drove the increase in take rate from 13–14% in 2023 to over 20% in 2024–2025?

Almost entirely the revenue recognition change described above, rather than a change in pricing or unit economics. Because take rate is revenue divided by GMV, moving delivery fees out of cost of sales and into revenue raises the numerator without touching the denominator, so the audited series understates the earlier years relative to the current methodology.

On the consistent restated basis the progression is 17% in 2023, 18% in 2024 and 20% in 2025 — a steady annual step, consistent with the maturing merchant mix and higher delivery fee recovery over the period, rather than a step change. The 2026 plan assumes 20.3%, in line with seven months of actuals.

Data room: Take Rate Trend 2023–2025, explanatory note.

The intangible originates in the 2019 seed round. 07Do driver wallets pay restaurants up front? Do captains deposit to merchant POS with net settlement? Cash-point settlement?

Net settlement, on cash orders. The driver pays the merchant the net order value — gross order value less BeeOrder’s commission — at pickup, then collects the full order value plus the delivery fee from the customer on delivery. The commission therefore sits with the driver, who periodically deposits cash at authorised cash points, and the company collects and reconciles those deposits.

Drivers pre-fund a digital wallet, which is what allows them to pay merchants on collection. The company takes its margin at the point of pickup rather than waiting on merchant settlement, so working capital does not scale with order volume.

17How do we determine 65% market share?

BeeOrder conducts a periodic assessment of the competitive landscape which involves collecting sample data from selected restaurants on order volume through BeeOrder relative to competitors. As of August 2026 the company estimates that it holds 75% of the app-based delivery market across the five cities in which it operates. This is a management estimate rather than an audited figure.

18I understand $350k per dark store but see $83k of capex in the internal model. I may be misreading.

You were reading it correctly, and the model has since been rebuilt. The $83,000 was a narrow capital expenditure definition covering equipment and fit-out only, which sat inconsistently with the all-in figure quoted elsewhere.

The current model uses $316,000 per store, fully loaded — fit-out, cold chain and backup power, technology and integration hardware, recruitment and training, permits, opening consumables, the launch campaign, approximately $80,000 of opening inventory and a $100,000 two-year rent prepayment. City hub build costs and the Yango implementation sit alongside this rather than inside it.

The current dark store assumptions and projections come from a full operational planning exercise carried out in August 2026. The rollout is rule-driven rather than calendar-driven: a store opens only once the previous store reaches 800 orders per day.

19How does management think about e-payment adoption relative to the cash-cycle moat?

Our approach is market-driven and phased rather than abrupt. We expect the transition to electronic payments to happen gradually, following customer behaviour, merchant readiness and the broader market evolution. In the short to medium term cash is expected to remain the dominant payment method in our market, so we continue to invest in and optimise our cash operations. As adoption increases we will expand our digital payment offering accordingly, ensuring a smooth transition without disrupting the existing ecosystem.
